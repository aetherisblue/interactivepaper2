The elementary object is the matrix of complete dimensional meetings
$$R(m,n)=\frac{n^n}{m^m}.$$
Each off-diagonal pair $  R(m,n)  $ and $  R(n,m)  $ is related by the reciprocal identity
$$R(m,n)\cdot R(n,m)=1.$$
2. Native logarithmic reading
Choose any positive entry $  r=R(m,n)  $ as the base of a logarithm. Then
$$\log_r(r)=1,\qquad\log_r\bigl(\tfrac{1}{r}\bigr)=-1.$$
The multiplicative relation “product equals 1” appears in this base as the additive relation “the two values differ from zero by a unit step and are opposite in sign.”

A longer note on the internal structure of the reciprocal scale matrix and the paths that did not lead here
Abstract
The matrix of pure dimensional meetings $  R(m,n)=n^n/m^m  $ carries a simple but consequential internal correspondence: when any elementary entry $  r  $ is chosen as its own logarithmic base, the reciprocal pair $  (r,1/r)  $ becomes the unit additive step $  (+1,-1)  $. This note develops the correspondence and, with equal care, records the principal approaches that were tried and discarded on the way to it. The failures are instructive; each one clarifies why the native-base reading is the minimal structure that actually works.
1. The finished matrix
We begin with the matrix whose entries are the pure meetings of complete dimensional scales:
$$R(m,n)=\frac{n^n}{m^m}.$$
The diagonal is identically 1. Every off-diagonal pair satisfies the reciprocal identity
$$R(m,n)\cdot R(n,m)=1.$$
This matrix is treated as the finished catalogue of elementary dimensional meetings. All further structure is read from it rather than imposed upon it.
2. The successful correspondence
Fix any positive entry $  r=R(m,n)  $. Taking the logarithm with base $  r  $ itself yields
$$\log_r(r)=1,\qquad\log_r(1/r)=-1.$$
The multiplicative statement “product equals 1” is thereby translated into the additive statement “the two values form a unit step about zero.”
Consequences follow at once:

Every elementary pool can serve as its own unit reference frame.
The elementary act of additive communication between a pool and its complement is identical with the reciprocal relation read in the matching base.
Addition (as unit communication) and the reciprocal are two registers of the same underlying identity.

3. Approaches that did not work
3.1 Explosive dimensional meeting as a binary operation
An early attempt defined a meeting operation on dimensional dual pairs by sending labels $  (k,\ell)  $ to the product $  k\ell  $ and scaling by the full tower $  (k\ell)^{k\ell}  $. The operation was dimensionally faithful but numerically catastrophic: after two or three iterations the normalized values underflowed to zero while the duals overflowed to infinity.
Why it failed: the growth rate of $  (k\ell)^{k\ell}  $ outruns any useful bookkeeping. The operation could not be iterated and therefore could not serve as a practical composition law. Logarithmic duals delayed the collapse by only a single step; they did not remove the underlying violence of the tower.
3.2 Continuous interpolation of the explosive meeting
A continuous parameter $  t  $ was introduced so that the discrete meeting became a path, and the derivative at $  t=0  $ was computed. An explicit infinitesimal meeting rate was obtained.
Why it failed as a foundation: the derivative existed, yet it remained a formal directional rate on an abstract space of dual pairs. It did not reconnect to the original matrix entries in a way that clarified energy movement or communication between concrete pools. The construction was mathematically correct but conceptually downstream of the simpler native-base observation.
3.3 Energy pools without internal reference frames
A picture was developed in which energy occupies pools separated by operators (addition as free communication, multiplication as a wall). The picture was evocative and consistent with conservation via reciprocals, but it lacked a mechanism that could turn costly transitions into free ones.
Why it remained incomplete: without the ability to choose a native logarithmic base drawn from the matrix itself, the distinction between “wall” and “free communication” stayed absolute and external. The later discovery that a native base can convert a multiplicative jump into a unit additive step supplies precisely the missing internal mechanism.
3.4 Treating the matrix as a static lookup table for data embedding
Several attempts used $  R(m,n)  $ merely as a dictionary of reference scales for embedding ordinary numbers.
Why it was insufficient: the approach never engaged the reciprocal identity as a generative relation, nor did it explain why logarithms of different bases produce qualitatively different communicative behavior. It remained a passive representational tool rather than an active structural account.
3.5 Forcing geometric interpretations too early
Circles, loops, radii, and curvature were repeatedly tried as immediate readings of the scales $  k^k  $.
Why they were set aside: each geometric reading added interpretive load without clarifying the algebraic or energetic relations inside the matrix. The native-base correspondence required no geometric commitment; it lives entirely at the level of the numerical and reciprocal structure. Geometry may later be recovered, but it is not required for the unit-step observation.
4. Why the native-base reading succeeds where the others did not
It stays inside the original matrix.
It uses only the reciprocal identity that the matrix already possesses.
It introduces no explosive auxiliary operation.
It converts the multiplicative structure into additive unit communication by a change of reference frame that the matrix itself supplies.
It therefore requires no external scaffolding and generates a concrete, iterable notion of elementary energy movement (the unit step).
5. Open directions that remain legitimate

Systematic classification of which native bases simplify which families of transitions.
Paths composed entirely of unit steps with successive base updates.
The interaction of native bases with higher-dimensional meetings beyond the simple reciprocal pair.
Possible return to geometric readings once the communicative structure is stable.

The reciprocal scale matrix contains, for every elementary entry, a natural logarithmic frame in which that entry and its complement become the unit of communication. Earlier approaches failed either by introducing uncontrollable growth, by remaining too formal, by lacking an internal reference mechanism, or by imposing external interpretations too soon. The native-base correspondence avoids each of these defects and rests directly on the reciprocal identity the matrix already carries.
